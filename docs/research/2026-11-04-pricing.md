# Pricing Oort Cloud: competitor prices, our unit costs, and three candidate structures

Date: 2026-11-04
Status: research note for Marcus to accept or reject item by item (see the last section)
Question: what should Oort Cloud charge so that solo developers and small teams pay little, Marcus would pay it himself, and gross margin holds at scale?

All prices below were read from the cited URL on 2026-11-04 (a WebFetch summary of the page, not a screenshot). Anything I could not read from a primary source is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Inputs read first: design doc §9 (cloud stack, pricing line) and §14 (lifecycle), and the draft `plans` array in `/Users/mjnong/Documents/ort-marketing-mobile-dashboard/src/lib/oort-data.ts`.

## Summary

- **The draft plans do not cover cost.** Our all-in managed cost is about **$0.089 per 1,000 `observe`** and **$0.064 per 1,000 `recall`** (section 4). The draft Pro plan ($49, 500k retrievals + 100k writes) costs about $41 to serve if fully used, so gross margin is about 15% at full use. Its overage ($0.06 per 1k retrievals, $0.10 per 1k writes) is at or below cost for retrievals.
- **Voyage rerank is 93% of recall cost.** Voyage counts rerank tokens as query tokens × documents + document tokens, so one recall over 30 × 80-token docs is about 3,000 tokens. Without rerank, recall costs about $0.0044 per 1,000. Rerank must be optional, conditional, or priced separately.
- **Haiku 5.5 is cheap** ($0.10 in / $0.50 out per MTok under 100k-token prompts) and Jev is cheaper still ($0.042 per MTok input, output free). The model bill per observe is about $0.08 per 1,000; the infra bill is about $0.006.
- **Fixed infra is small**: Clerk Pro $25 + Convex Pro $25 per developer + Atlas Flex up to $30 is about $80 per month before any customer. The risk is not fixed cost; it is the warm tier. Production Atlas Vector Search wants M10 plus separate search nodes (price **UNVERIFIED** above S20 at $0.12/hour).
- **Competitors**: Zep Flex $125 per month for 50k credits; Supermemory Pro $19 with $20 of credits; Letta Pro $20; Cognee $1.00 per 1M tokens; Mem0 now publishes only Free and Custom (no dollar prices). A $19 Pro tier matches Supermemory and is 6.6x cheaper than Zep.
- **Recommendation** is in the last section: Structure A as the default, with the BYO-keys discount of Structure B as a switch on the same plans.

## 4. Marginal cost per 1,000 operations and per GB

### Assumptions (change these and the numbers move)

1. Jev decision call per observe: **about 600 input tokens** (observation text plus a short context). The Jev page gives price per token, not per call, so 600 is my assumption.
2. Writer: 25% of observes pass the gate; each writer call is 1,500 input + 150 output tokens on Haiku 5.5 (under the 100k-token price tier). No prompt caching assumed.
3. One embedding of 60 tokens per observe (conservative: all observes embed, even those not reaching the writer).
4. Recall: query of about 20 tokens; one query embedding; one rerank of 30 documents × 80 tokens.
5. Convex function calls: 3 per observe (action, mutation, query) and 2 per recall, billed at the Professional overage rate of $2 per 1M. This overcounts, since 25M calls are included in the $25 plan. Atlas read/write cost per operation is treated as fixed (cluster price) not marginal.
6. Memory size: about 2.5 KB per stored memory (about 1 KB encrypted text and metadata, 1 KB quantized embedding, 0.5 KB index overhead), so 1 GB holds about 400,000 memories. UNVERIFIED; measure it in Phase 1.

## 6. Which metering unit is easiest to explain to non-technical buyers

1. **Active end users** (Structure C): easiest, because buyers already count users; weakest for a single developer using an agent, and needs a fair-use cap to stop a few heavy users blowing the margin.
2. **Operations** (one operation = one `observe` or one `recall`): second easiest. A blended $0.075 per 1k ops cost works as one number (about 41% to 57% margin at $0.15 per 1k), and removes the "what is a retrieval vs a write" question. It hides that observes cost 38% more than recalls, which only matters if mixes are extreme.
3. **Observes and recalls separately** (Structures A and B, and the draft page's "retrievals" and "writes"): accurate to cost, but "observe" and "recall" are Oort vocabulary; the marketing page already says "retrievals" and "writes", which Mem0 ("adds", "retrievals") also uses.
4. **Credits** (Zep, Supermemory, Graphlit): hardest to forecast, because the price per action varies with text length; avoid.

## 7. Recommendation (for Marcus to accept or reject)

1. **Ship Structure A**, with Pro at **$19** and a hard-stop Free tier (5k observes, 10k recalls) to cap worst-case free cost at about $1 per account, rather than the draft's $4.11. Cut the Developer free tier from 50k retrievals.
2. **Offer BYO keys (Structure B) as a per-org setting** on every plan, billed at the B rates, defaulting on for `zero-knowledge` mode; this is Marcus's own "I would happily pay $9" tier and the best P3 answer (77% margin vs 51%).
3. **Make rerank optional per `recall`** (default on for the first N per month, or only when fusion confidence is low); without it, recall costs $0.0044 per 1,000 and a Supermemory-style $5 per 1M price becomes possible. Keep the Convex-only warm tier as default; offer Atlas only above a size threshold, priced at a higher warm rate (the draft $0.25 per GB loses money on Atlas).
